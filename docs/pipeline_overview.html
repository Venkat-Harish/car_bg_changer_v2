Stage 1Stage 2

Getting the right photos, and hiding the number plate

1
Pick frames from the video
What we do
Take a clean still from the video at regular intervals as we walk around the car.
Problem solved
A video is thousands of near-identical frames. We need a small, usable set of photos.
➜
2
Identify which side of the car each photo shows
What we do
Label each photo as front, rear, left, right or a corner view, with how sure we are.
Problem solved
Later steps must know the viewpoint to place the car the right way round in the studio.
➜
3
Find the number plate
What we do
Locate every number plate in every photo, including small or angled ones.
Problem solved
Plates must be hidden for privacy and replaced with dealer branding.
4
Find the plate's exact outline
What we do
Trace the plate's true four corners, even when it is tilted or seen at an angle.
Problem solved
A plain rectangle looks stuck-on when the plate is angled. The cover must fit the plate exactly.
➜
5
Replace the plate with the brand image
What we do
Lay the dealer's brand image onto the plate, bent to its exact shape and angle.
Problem solved
Every photo carries consistent branding and no registration number is visible.
➜
6
Match the brightness to the plate
What we do
Adjust the brand image to the plate's real lighting, for every plate colour:
WHITE YELLOW RED GREEN BLACK BLUE
Problem solved
A bright sticker on a shaded plate looks fake. It must blend in, and must not flicker in video.

Stage 3

Putting the car in a studio

7
Cut the car out of the photo
What we do
Separate the car from everything around it: showroom, street, people, walls.
Problem solved
The car has to be lifted off its original scene before it can go anywhere new.
➜
8
Refine the cut-out
What we do
Keep only the main car, fill in the windows, remove stray bits and tidy the edges.
Problem solved
Raw cut-outs have holes, ragged edges and leftover clutter that look unprofessional.
➜
9
Remove other vehicles
Optional
What we do
Pick out this car and drop anything stuck to it: a car parked behind, a person, a gate.
Problem solved
In busy showrooms and parking lots, neighbouring cars get cut out together with ours.
➜
10
Create the studio backgrounds
Built once
What we do
Turn each background picture (white turntable studio, grey studio, race track) into a 3D room.
Problem solved
A flat picture can only be seen from one angle. A 3D room can be viewed from any photo's angle.
11
Work out the car's angle and the camera's position
What we do
Using the car model's real size and the photo itself, find how the car is turned and where the camera stood.
Problem solved
A car shot from above, pasted onto a floor seen at eye level, looks like it is floating.
➜
12
Place the car in the 3D studio
What we do
View the studio from that same camera, add a natural shadow and floor reflection, and set the car on top.
Problem solved
The car must sit naturally on the floor. The car itself is never altered, so its true condition stays visible.
➜
13
Studio-ready photos
What we deliver
One finished image per photo for each studio background, and optionally a new studio video of the car.
Problem solved
Consistent, branded, showroom-quality listings from a single phone video.
